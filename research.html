Research Interests

My research aims to predict and prevent locomotion injuries early by evaluating established biomechanical risk indicators at the individual level.

As a research assistant, I investigated kinematics, kinetics, neuromuscular mechanics, and passive mechanics across a wide range of age groups, from children to older adults. My experimental experience spans various motor tasks, from fundamental broad jumps to complex paradigms like dual-task walking and repeated hopping under perturbations.

Using statistical comparison methods based on group averages to examine differences across individuals, I kept discovering that even individuals exposed to identical demands showed highly variable responses. This experience convinced me that injury risk cannot be fully explained by group averages alone. I aim to analyze this complexity of human movement using machine learning alongside traditional statistics, representing each person's risk profile in a nonlinear, individualized form — ultimately building predictive tools that can intervene before injury occurs.

Core Focus Areas

1) Investigating the neuromuscular and biomechanical mechanisms underlying human locomotion and performance.

2) Understanding individual differences in how these neuromechanical responses evolve across repeated exposures over time.

3) Developing interpretable, machine-learning-based models that translate these mechanisms into individualized injury risk prediction — with the long-term goal of building field-ready, wearable-based screening tools.
